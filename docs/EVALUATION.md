> **How to read the LLM rows.** The judge scores come from a single judge (`qwen3:8b`, run locally) on a 64-issue gold set (vscode n=11, k8s n=53) replayed from a recorded cassette, so CI runs make zero live LLM calls. n=11 gives vscode a wide band (the regression gate fires at −0.45 for vscode, −0.22 for k8s), and the grounding ratchet (≤1/53, ADR-0061) is currently met with 0/53, which sits inside the same Wilson interval as 1/53 ([0.3, 9.9]% vs [0.0, 6.8]%). The previous rows (10.26/15 k8s, 8.64/15 vscode, floor-fail 18.9% / 63.6%, fabrication 0/53) were measured with a different judge and the retired `llama-3.1-8b-instant` on a different gold set; they are **not comparable** and were removed from the table (they remain in git history and in [`docs/EVALUATION_HISTORY.md`](EVALUATION_HISTORY.md)). The classifier retrain moved the judge mean −0.156 (95% CI [−0.51, +0.20], n=64) against the voided pre-retrain run: no quality-improvement claim is made (ADR-0061). The 2026-10-11 re-record (ADR-0064) changed three things together (the median point, the 'historical median' label, the calibrated interval in the prompt) and re-sampled the synthesis: pooled mean 12.03 → 12.09, vscode 12.27 → 12.73 (at the edge of its 0.45 band, not claimed as an improvement), k8s 11.98 → 11.96. `resolution_estimate_reasonableness` fell (1.67 → 1.58, see the row above); it is an LLM proxy for how the estimate reads, the drop is not significant and is confounded, and the median-point decision rests on ground truth (median absolute error 3.52d vs 7.28d), so it is recorded as an unresolved signal, not a reason to revert.
>
> **What the grounding numbers count, and what they do not.** `fabrication_rate` and the grounding ratchet count plans where `grounding_status.all_grounded` is false: the predicted component is not in the classifier's own top-3, or a cited similar-issue number was not in the retrieved set. That is a **consistency check against the pipeline's upstream outputs, not a correctness check against gold labels**: 20 of the 64 plans predict a component that is wrong versus gold, and all 20 are scored "grounded" because the wrong guess still falls inside the classifier's top-3 (recomputed on the 2026-10-11 cassette with `scripts/measure_grounding.py` and the gold labels in `eval/eval_set.jsonl`; method: ADR-0058 addendum). `0/53` and `0/11` must therefore not be read as error rates. The report-only metric that tracks the LLM overriding the classifier is `component_departed_from_top1_rate` (5 of 64 plans; all 5 wrong vs gold).
>
> **Component accuracy: the deterministic number is 44/64 = 68.8%; the judge's `component_match` is more lenient.** The number to quote is exact string equality between the plan's predicted component and the gold label: **44 of 64 plans (68.8%, Wilson 95% [56.6, 78.8])**, computed without any model by `scripts/component_match_agreement.py` from the committed cassette (`reports/component_match_agreement_2026-10-16.json`). The judge's `component_match` (the LLM-judge rows above, kept as reported) is a comparison of the plan's component with the gold component printed in its own prompt (2 correct, 1 plausible but not gold, 0 wrong), not an inference from the issue text, and it is lenient on hierarchical neighbours: it scored 2 for all 44 exact matches and also for **5 of the 20 wrong plans**, all kubernetes near-labels (e.g. gold `cloudprovider` vs predicted `provider/openstack`; the others are `system-requirement`/`kubelet`, `usability`/`kubectl` twice, `swagger`/`api`); of the other 15 wrong plans it scored 11 as 1 and 4 as 0. Agreement with "equal iff 2" is 59/64 = 92.2%, and every disagreement is the judge being more lenient. Consequences: the judge's mean `component_match` (1.70 of 2) is higher than strict equality would give (1.38), and the judge's worst band (`component_match` = 0, one of the two inputs to the floor-fail rate) caught only 4 of the 20 wrong components, so **the floor-fail rate understates component misses**.
>
> **Resolution rows.** The point estimate is the training-window median (owner decision D7, [ADR-0064](architecture/adr/0064-train-median-point-estimate.md): k8s 3.04 d, vscode 3.84 d). The learned model did not beat it on the typical issue in either repo (median absolute error); on k8s it is marginally better on mean error (+2%), almost entirely from the longest-resolving 20% of issues (598 issues, 113+ days; they carry 95.9% of the gain, `reports/k8s_gain_tail.json`). The learned parts in production are the k8s bucket classifier and the conformal intervals. Where this came from: the learned point estimates had a train/serve skew (the `emb_*` features were zero-filled at serving, found 2026-10-07, DECISION_LOG D17); the k8s side was fixed by reusing the retrieval query embedding (PR #150, ADR-0062) and the point estimate was then replaced by the median because the median is the better answer for the typical issue. The vscode intervals use the median as their centre and the stored CQR adjustment; the 82.7% coverage is measured on one reconstructed 7-day window (2026-04-21..27, chronological 40/60 split by `created_at`), where the 2015–16 training median (3.84 d) is far above the test median (0.049 d), so the interval's lower bound collapses toward zero and it is effectively an upper bound; coverage is a measurement on that window, not a guarantee. The numbers this replaces (k8s "as served" +0.71%, vscode −54.2% worse than naive, vscode conformal coverage 74.6%, k8s 80.81%/76.2%) were measured on the pre-D7 served state and remain in DECISION_LOG D17–D19 and git history.
